We are a six-person studio and we moved to NovaNote after a failed attempt to build our own wiki. Migration was the thing that broke us. There was no official importer for our old workspace, so I had to scrape pages manually and reformat them. I spent two weekends doing that, and a third fixing broken links and orphaned images. For a company that prides itself on speed, that was embarrassing.

Once we were in, the basics worked well. The editor is smooth, keyboard shortcuts are thoughtful, and our engineers loved the markdown support. Collaboration was fine for small edits, but comments were shallow. There is no way to assign a comment or turn a discussion into a task without copying the text around, and in a busy studio that meant decisions got lost.

Performance stayed acceptable while we were under roughly two thousand blocks, but our clients demanded embedded prototypes and large media files. Pages with many embeds became unusable on older laptops. Half the team is on Macs from 2019, and NovaNote drained them. We started keeping heavy items in a separate tool just to keep main docs fast, which defeated the entire purpose.

Migration again: when we tried to leave, export was incomplete. Some callouts and tables came out flattened. That damaged trust more than anything else. A tool that makes it easy to leave earns loyalty on the way in, but NovaNote made leaving painful on purpose, or so it felt.

Customer support did not help. Every message took at least two days, and one bug about nested tables was closed as won't fix. Our final verdict: the core product is nice, but for a team that needs to move fast, being locked into a slow app with weak import and export is a liability. We now use a combination of Notion and a lightweight internal site.
