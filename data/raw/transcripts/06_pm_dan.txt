I lead product for a forty-person scaleup, and NovaNote was our company wiki for almost a year. The collapse was slow until it was sudden. Early on, everything glowed. The editor was fast, search was quick, and our engineering team adopted it without a single complaint. We built detailed specs, RFCs and decision logs, and NovaNote made it feel effortless.

Collaboration is where the product fails at scale. Real-time editing is fine for two people, but with a team of forty you get constant merge collisions. There is no fine-grained permission for a document, only workspace-wide settings, so an intern can delete a page that senior staff authored. We lost a data-schema doc once, recovered from a backup, and everyone lost confidence. Concurrency and access control are not nice-to-haves, they are the core contract of a shared knowledge base.

Performance followed the same curve. At first everything loaded instantly, but once we crossed a few thousand pages with heavy embeds, navigation slowed and the command palette lagged. People started hoarding links in Slack because opening NovaNote felt slow. A wiki is only as good as its performance at its actual size, and NovaNote did not scale with us.

We tried support, and support was courteous but shallow. Two of our fastest issues were closed as known bugs with no timeline. For a business tool that we host our source decisions in, that answer is not enough.

We moved to a more structured platform with proper permissions and revision history. It cost more, but the reliability is worth it. My honest recommendation to NovaNote: nail collaboration permissions and document-level performance before adding any new feature.
